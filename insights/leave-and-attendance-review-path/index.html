Leave and attendance: why time inputs need their own review path

Most salary queries start as a time question. Why leave, attendance and holiday calendars deserve a proper review path, and what both sides of a leave request need.

Ask a payroll team where salary queries come from and many will point to the same place: not the calculation, but the time inputs. A leave day recorded twice, an attendance gap that was never corrected, a holiday that applied to one office but not another. By the time these reach payroll, they look like payroll errors.

That is why time inputs deserve their own review path, before they become a number on a payslip.

Why time inputs are easy to get wrong

Leave and attendance are recorded by many people, every day, often informally. An employee mentions a sick day in a message. A manager approves leave verbally. Someone working at a client site forgets to mark attendance. Each of these is small. Together, at month end, they become a pile of exceptions that someone has to reconcile under time pressure.

The underlying issue is that time records are treated as a side input to payroll instead of a record in their own right, with their own rules, approvals and history.

Three parts of a proper review path

Leave policies and balances

A leave request only makes sense against a policy and a balance. If the balance lives in a spreadsheet and the policy in a handbook, every approval becomes a separate conversation. When policies and balances sit next to the request, the approver can see whether it fits before saying yes.

Holiday calendars

Organisations with teams in different locations often observe different holidays. If the calendar is wrong, attendance looks wrong, and leave may be deducted for a day that was a holiday. Maintaining the relevant holiday calendars as part of the system, rather than as a note, removes a common source of disputes.

Attendance and regularisation

Attendance records will always have gaps: a missed punch, a field visit, a system outage. Regularisation is the process of correcting them. A good regularisation path has a request, a reason, a reviewer and a recorded outcome, so a correction is visible rather than silently edited.

Both sides of a leave request

A leave request has two people in it, and both need something different.

  • The employee needs a place to check their balance, raise a request, see its status and understand the outcome without chasing anyone.
  • The manager needs a clearer way to review requests: the balance, the policy and any overlap with the team, in one view.

Picture a hypothetical employee in a Chennai office who spends a day at a client site and does not mark attendance. She raises a regularisation request with a short reason. Her manager reviews it alongside her attendance for the period and approves it. The corrected record and the approval are both kept. When payroll inputs are reviewed later, the day is already explained.

What HRMS lets you explore

The leave and attendance capability in HRMS covers:

  • Leave policies, balances and requests.
  • Holiday calendars.
  • Attendance periods and regularisation requests.
  • Employee portal screens, so the employee and manager sides of a request can both be previewed.

The source review recorded leave, holiday calendars, and attendance and regularisation as implemented. In a walkthrough, ask how approved time records reach the payroll inputs in the version you are evaluating, and what a payroll reviewer sees of them.

Current availability: The evaluation uses demo personas, so production authentication for employees and managers is a separate requirement. Location-based features are partial and need deployment-specific permission and privacy checks; HRMS does not offer automatic tracking on every device. The mobile client exists as source, but native-device execution was not verified in the review.

Why a separate path helps payroll

When time inputs are reviewed where they happen, by the people who know the facts, payroll receives fewer surprises. The month-end conversation shifts from "is this right?" to "this was approved on this date, by this person, for this reason". That is the same trail that answers an employee who asks why their salary changed.

It also spreads the work. Instead of the payroll team reconciling every exception at the end of the month, managers and employees resolve most of them during the month, with a record of each decision.

What to ask to see

  • One leave request from both sides: the employee raising it and the manager reviewing it.
  • A leave balance before and after an approved request.
  • A holiday calendar and how it affects attendance for a period.
  • A regularisation request with its reason, reviewer and outcome.
  • Where these records appear when payroll inputs are reviewed.

If you manage a team or are an employee yourself, the page for managers and employees describes this side of the product.

The takeaway

Most salary questions start as time questions. Give leave, attendance and holiday calendars a review path of their own, with both sides of each request visible, and fewer of those questions reach payroll unexplained. To test this, bring one anonymised leave or attendance case to a sample-data walkthrough.
