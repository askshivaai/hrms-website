Spreadsheets and message threads vs one connected HR record

Spreadsheets are not the enemy, and a connected system is not automatically better. An honest look at who each approach suits, and the signs it is time to change.

Many organisations run people operations on a familiar combination: an employee spreadsheet, leave approvals in message threads or email, attendance in another sheet, and a separate payroll tool or service. Others keep employee records, time and payroll review in one connected system.

Neither is right for everyone. This is a fair comparison of the two approaches, written by people who build the second one, so read it with that in mind.

Approach 1: spreadsheets, message threads and a separate payroll tool

Where it works well

  • Familiar and flexible. Everyone already knows how to use a spreadsheet. Adding a column takes seconds.
  • Low setup. There is nothing to configure, migrate or integrate before you start.
  • Specialist payroll. A dedicated payroll tool or service may already handle parts of the process, such as salary transfers or returns, that an HR record does not.
  • Clear at small scale. With a small, stable team and one person who knows everything, the arrangement can be perfectly adequate.

Where it struggles

  • Context is split. HR handles the record, a manager approves the leave in a message, finance checks the payroll in another tool. A simple question moves between several people.
  • History lives in memory. When a value is edited in a sheet, the old value and the reason for the change are usually gone.
  • Versions drift. Two copies of the attendance sheet, a revised salary in one file but not the other.
  • Reconstruction is slow. Explaining a result means searching messages and comparing files.

Approach 2: one connected record

Where it works well

  • Context stays together. The employee record, time inputs, adjustments and rule versions can be reviewed in one place.
  • History is part of the record. Changes are recorded as events rather than overwritten.
  • Review stages are explicit. Input review, calculation, approval and locking happen at known points, by known people.
  • Requests are easier to follow. Employees and managers see the same request and its status.

Where it struggles

  • Setup effort. Policies, calendars, salary structures and rules have to be configured before the system is useful.
  • Change for people. Managers and employees have to adopt a new place for requests.
  • Dependence on one system. Access control, data handling and availability all matter more when everything is in one place.
  • Connected is not the same as correct. A connected record makes errors easier to trace. It does not stop wrong rules or wrong inputs.

Who each approach suits

Spreadsheets and threads may be enough if

  • Your team is small and changes rarely.
  • One or two people hold the full picture and are reliably available.
  • Salary queries are rare and quick to answer.
  • Your payroll provider already covers what you need.

A connected record is worth evaluating if

  • Headcount is growing, and informal approvals and scattered records are getting harder to manage.
  • Answering "why has my salary changed?" regularly takes several people.
  • Rule changes, revisions and corrections are frequent enough that memory is no longer reliable.
  • Managers need a clearer way to review requests.

Many organisations will sit in between, and a mixed arrangement is a legitimate choice: a connected record for people, time and payroll review, with a specialist provider for the steps it does not cover.

Where HRMS stands today

HRMS is a connected-record approach. It brings employee details, leave, attendance and payroll review into one platform, with a history of recorded events and rule versions. It is designed to help a team trace a result back to the records and rules behind it.

Current availability: HRMS is offered for sample-data evaluation only. Production authentication and independent payroll-rule validation remain outstanding. No verified bank-disbursement or statutory-filing connection was established, so even with a connected record you would still need a separate route for salary transfers and returns. No public price is listed.

That means the honest comparison today is not "replace everything". It is: would a connected record for people, time and payroll review improve how your team explains results, alongside the tools you would keep?

How to decide

  1. Write down the last three people-related questions that took too long to answer.
  2. For each, note how many people and files were involved.
  3. Ask whether a connected history would have changed that.
  4. If the answer is yes, test it with one anonymised workflow before anything else.

Our guide to evaluating HR software with sample data explains how. The product overview shows what the connected record covers, and the page for business owners looks at the growth signals in more detail.

The takeaway

Spreadsheets are not a failure, and a connected system is not automatically an upgrade. The right choice depends on how often your team has to explain a result and how much of that explanation lives in people's memory. If it is often, and a lot, a connected record deserves a careful evaluation. Either way, the decision is easier when it rests on one real example rather than a general impression.
